## U1: OSRS Grand Exchange flip advisor (Market 3/10)

**Demand signals.**
- Flipping is a large activity. On the RuneLite Plugin Hub, **Flipping Utilities has 74,726 active installs**, **Flipping Copilot 8,830**, GE Filters 6,355 and Flipper2 5,015 ([RuneLite Plugin Hub](https://runelite.net/plugin-hub)).
- Flipping Copilot's site claims **20M+ flips, 1.4T gp of profit and a "90% success rate"** ([flippingcopilot.com](https://flippingcopilot.com/)).
- Players do pay real money, but only small amounts. **GE Tracker Premium costs £2.00/month** (£20/year) and covers an unlimited flip finder, alerts, a profit tracker and API access ([GE Tracker pricing](https://www.ge-tracker.com/pricing)).
- Flipping Copilot now runs a **free plan plus a paid upgrade** that adds the "members item list and dump alerts". I could not retrieve its exact price (unverified).
- Flippo.club sells a "Pro" tier for faster RuneLite trade sync ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/cjjepheabgaehcgodkidjcljdjcdgodd)).

**Competitors.**
- Flipping Copilot (free plus premium; an in-client AI that suggests item, price and quantity, which is literally this idea).
- Flipping Utilities (free).
- GE Tracker (£2/mo).
- prices.runescape.wiki (free).
- Others: Flippo.club (freemium), osrstools.net GE tracker (free), Discord flipping bots (Flopper on top.gg), and many free hobby sites.
- Total is about 10–15 tools, with 3 that matter.

**Bond value: what the gp a tool earns is worth in dollars.** An Old School Bond costs **$9.99** (£7.49 / €8.99). The price rose for the third time on 10 March 2026 ([OSRS Wiki](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Old_school_bond)). At about 12.06M gp per bond, **1M gp is worth about $0.83 of membership**. If a tool adds 2M gp/hour, the player gains roughly $1.65/hour in bond value. Most of the value a tool creates stays as in-game gp, so the price a player will pay in real dollars is capped near GE Tracker's £2/mo. The brief's $4–6/mo is above market.

**Saturation: High.** To get noticed, a newcomer would need to show it beats Flipping Copilot's free tier on verified gp/hour. The user's own backtest found that a naive spread scanner *loses* money after the 2% tax. The incumbent also runs inside the game client, and the newcomer would not.

**AI commoditization: Low** from ChatGPT, because a chatbot has no live prices. That does not help a newcomer, though: the "AI flip advisor" position is already taken.

**Market size check.** $300/mo needs about 60–75 subscribers at $4–5, or about 140 at GE Tracker's price. That number exists within roughly 90k plugin installs. Winning those subscribers from a free in-client incumbent does not look realistic.

**Wedge, if any.**
- Mobile and non-RuneLite players: Copilot and Flipping Utilities are RuneLite-only. However, GE Tracker already has a mobile app.
- An "update-speculation / investing" newsletter (items likely to move on Jagex news). This is a content product, not a tool.
- Both are thin. **Not recommended as a business.** It is fine as a personal hobby.

Maayot ("Pro" tier with teacher feedback, "thousands of users", 100+ iOS and 150+ Android reviews) has a price I could not confirm, roughly $10/mo (unverified) ([maayot](https://www.maayot.com/)).

**Is there open demand for the briefed product, a reader matched to your known words?** Mostly no. It is already built:
- **Read Bean** assesses your level and tailors lessons.
- **maayot** reviews "at-risk words in context".
- **Mandarin Mosaic** (free) gives i+1 sentences matched to your tracked vocabulary.
- **Readibu** turns any web page into graded reading.
- **HSKStory** covers HSK 1–9.
- A free **Obsidian "Chinese Comprehensible Input" plugin** generates AI stories.
- Any learner can prompt ChatGPT for "a story with HSK 3 words".

Sources: [hskstory comparison](https://hskstory.com/guides/best-chinese-reading-apps), [Obsidian plugin](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/chinese-comprehensible-input). **Saturation: Red ocean** for general Mandarin and **High** for AI graded readers. **AI-commoditization risk: High.**

**Underserved sub-niches, ranked.**
1. **HSK 3.0 transition (best).** This is the one with a dated catalyst.
   - CTI released the new syllabus in Nov 2025, with implementation from July 2026.
   - Regular 2026 sittings mostly stayed on HSK 2.0, and the formal switch date is unclear ([StudyCLI](https://studycli.org/hsk/the-new-hsk/)). At least one centre lists a **first official HSK 3.0 sitting on 13 Dec 2026** ([hsklord](https://hsklord.com/blog/hsk-3-0-complete-guide)).
   - Vocabulary targets change: 300, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 3,600 and 5,400 words for HSK 1–6. Learners and teachers need "what changed at my level" materials.
   - Proof of payment: a Gumroad seller lists **HSK 3.0 decks at $2.99 (L1) up to $29.99 (L7–9)** ([Gumroad](https://chlkdst.gumroad.com/l/ecvju)), and a free AnkiWeb HSK 3.0 deck exists.
   - What is still open: **level-matched graded stories and audio for the new 3.0 lists**, 2.0→3.0 delta decks, and mock-format practice.
   - This is exam-driven, so buyers have a deadline.
2. **Cantonese.** Clearly underserved. Duolingo and most major apps skip it, and Pandaist is one of the few graded readers ([Clozemaster blog](https://www.clozemaster.com/blog/?p=7174), [Nik Fuller](https://nikfuller.beehiiv.com/p/struggling-find-centralized-resources)). The catch is that the market is smaller and LLM-written Cantonese is weaker. A non-native operator cannot QA it, so the risk to trust is high.
3. **Adult heritage learners.** These learners speak but cannot read: "bilingual but not biliterate". Paid offerings mostly target children through live tutoring (LingoAce) ([LingoAce](https://www.lingoace.com/guides/article/heritage-learners-struggle-chinese-reading/)). An audio-first reader keyed on known *characters* fits this group. Demand evidence here is anecdotal (unverified size).
4. **Tones and listening.** This niche is getting crowded, with TonePerfect at $9.99/mo, Pinyin Trainer and others.
5. **Business Chinese.** Low evidence of self-serve digital demand. Buyers lean towards tutors and corporate programmes (unverified).

**Market size check.** $300/mo needs about 45 subscribers at $7, or about 30 deck sales a month at around $10. The learner base can support that. Getting found is the hard part.

**Recommendation.** Learning Chinese is a sensible *direction* only if the product is narrowed. Drop the generic "personalised reader" pitch. Lead with **HSK 3.0 transition products** (Gumroad and Anki decks plus graded story packs per new level, with a free AnkiWeb deck as the lead magnet), and treat the known-word reader as a later upsell. Add Cantonese only if a native reviewer is available.
