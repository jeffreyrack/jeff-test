## Q1: UltraWideo-class aged subscription extension (score 6)

**Demand signals (verified 2026-10-06).** The TrustMRR listing is still live at **$13,000** ("2.5× revenue multiple"), with $4,684 TTM, $346 MRR, 130 active subscriptions and $440 in the last 30 days (−8.9%). The monthly series matches the ideator exactly: Nov 2025 $212 through Sep 2026 $358. October shows $123 partway through the month. Paying users of the product category exist: 130 people pay $2.99/mo despite free alternatives. ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/ultrawideo.md))

**Competitors.** Zoom to Fill Ultrawide Video (200k+ users), Ultrawidify (free, open source), Ultrawide Fill and similar (~5–10 in total). Free OSS caps price increases.

**Acquisition-market check.** 2026 small-deal multiples are 2.5–4× revenue for sub-$1M ARR ([beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)). Extension listings show a median of ~4.4× profit ([exitbid](https://exitbid.io/blog/how-much-is-a-chrome-extension-worth-2026)). Bulk File Downloader is still active at $21,000 for $546/mo (~3.2× profit) ([Flippa](https://flippa.com/12839069)). The ideator's $7k offer (1.5× revenue) sits **well below market**, so the odds of acceptance are perhaps 25–35%. The −21% MRR trend and the spiky May–Jun months are the negotiating levers.

**Saturation:** Medium. **AI risk:** Medium. The code is trivially cloneable; the 70k installs and 591 reviews are not.

**Market size.** The asset already sits at ~$300–400/mo gross. Growth depends on in-product conversion, not market size.

**Wedge.** Placement inheritance, plus an Edge listing. The market is fine; the multiple is the problem, and the 12-month gate fails at any realistic price.

## Q2: Wheel of Life-class aged app (score 6)

**Demand signals (verified).** The listing is still live at **$11,111** (1.7× revenue), with $6,648 TTM, $181 MRR, 122 subscriptions and $552 in the last 30 days (+31%). There is a clear January spike ($830 in Dec, $1,063 in Jan). ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/wheel-of-life.md))

**Correction to the ideator.** The TrustMRR page labels the figure as **"verified net revenue from RevenueCat API (after store fees)"**. If that holds in due diligence, the ideator's 15% commission line double-counts about $80/mo. P50 net would then be ~$450, and payback at $6.5k would be ~15–16 months. That is better, but it still fails the 12-month gate.

**Revenue quality.** MRR of $181 against ~$480/mo average revenue means roughly 60% comes from annual and lifetime purchases. Lifetime revenue doesn't recur, so the ideator's "lifetime <40%" gate may fail.

**Competitors.** There are ~10+ App Store "wheel of life" or life-balance apps (unverified count). The free substitute is any chatbot running the exercise. This is the weakest product moat of the acquisitions.

**Timing.** This is the best-timed idea in the run: closing by about 1 Dec captures the Jan peak.

**Saturation:** Medium. **AI risk:** Medium-High, because AI clones are cheap and ChatGPT covers the core exercise.

**Wedge.** A Coach tier, plus the New Year in-app event.

## Q3: Standing ≤$3k fire-sale buy order (score 4)

**Evidence.** The archetype is confirmed **sold** at $3,000. The page is internally inconsistent: it lists $11,300 ARR but claims $1,250/mo profit, which would be $15k a year, more than revenue. Treat "fire-sale" listings' profit claims as unreliable. ([Microns](https://www.microns.io/startup-listings/ai-youtube-growth-platform))

**Market.** Medians are 2.5–4× revenue ([bigideasdb, 615 listings](https://bigideasdb.com/saas-valuation-multiples-2026)). A ≤0.55× revenue buy-box sits 5–7× below median, so qualifying deals will be rare and mostly decaying assets. The live sub-$5k inventory the ideator found (FigForm, Brevik, OUT23, Reelyzer) is mostly zero-revenue AI clones, which matches §19.

**Competitors.** Hundreds+ buyers and their bots watch TrustMRR, Microns and Flippa. Good deals clear in days.

**Saturation:** High on the buy side. **AI risk:** High, because AI-built clones dominate supply.

**Market size.** Expect perhaps 0–2 qualifying deals a quarter (unverified).

**Wedge.** Only speed plus strict screening. The 30-day pipeline test (≥3 desk-passes a month) is the right go/no-go. I expect it to fail.

## B1: HSK 3.0 speaking mock (score 4)

**Demand signals.** It is confirmed that L3–6 candidates must take the speaking test at the same level. But HSK 3.0 **began worldwide on 1 Jul 2026** after a 31 Jan 2026 trial ([hsklord](https://hsklord.com/blog/hsk-3-0-changes-2026); [Shanghai gov](https://edu.sh.gov.cn/study_en_hsk/20260107/5292266eca744a959c1db43237fbc185.html)). The "first sittings from 13 Dec" framing looks wrong, so the novelty window is already three months old.

**Competitors (~6–8):**
- **Official:** the official HSK Mock app and website mocks.
- **AI-native, all 2026 launches:** hsktest.ai (AI speaking assessment); HanSpik (released 14 May 2026, **0 ratings**, outside the top 30) ([mwm](https://mwm.ai/apps/hanspik/6769252925)); HSK Training.
- **Established:** Mandarin Temple ($19/mo, mocks included) and HSKK Test Prep.

HanSpik's zero ratings after 5 months suggests either that the audience is small or that organic discovery fails. Both cut against B1.

**Saturation:** Medium and rising. **AI risk:** High. This is §19 in action: several AI-built speaking-mock apps launched this year.

**Market size.** English-speaking L3–6 takers are a minority of candidates. Search volume for "HSK 3.0 speaking" is probably low hundreds a month (unverified). Reaching 30 sales a month needs most of that volume.

**Wedge.** Exam-date-keyed paid search plus creator affiliates. It is defensible only through distribution, and the ceiling is small.

## B2: SubWatch OSHA-history monitoring (score 4)

**Demand signals.** The need is real. OSHA cites GCs for subs' hazards under the multi-employer policy ([Parker Poe](https://www.parkerpoe.com/news/2022/04/osha-continues-to-cite-general-contractors-for-subcontractors)), and prequal forms ask for OSHA history ([SiteVetter blog](https://www.sitevetter.com/blog/osha-citation-lookup-contractor/)).

**Critical finding.** **SiteVetter already sells a product named "SubWatch — Compliance Monitoring"** ([pricing](https://www.sitevetter.com/pricing/)):

| SiteVetter plan | Price | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0/mo | 5 subs |
| Pro | $79/mo | 25 subs, 10 reports, then $12 per extra report |
| Business | $179/mo | Unlimited subs and reports |

- It covers OSHA, DOL, SAM.gov, EPA and CSLB licence, bond and comp data, with change alerts and a PDF compliance packet.
- It covers **California only**.

What this means for B2:
- B2's name, price, free "5 subs" hook and PDF are essentially already in market.
- The name must change (trademark and confusion risk).
- B2's $199/250-sub tier is undercut by SiteVetter's $179 unlimited tier.

**Other competitors:**
- HammerTech, Highwire, Avetta, ISNetworld and TradeTapp (enterprise);
- at least 4 Apify OSHA Actors and an OSHA MCP ([Apify](https://apify.com/pyralislabs/osha-violations-penalty-screener));
- a pay-per-call OSHA API ([2s.io](https://2s.io/gov/osha-violations)).

**Saturation:** Medium-High. **AI risk:** High, because the data is public and clones exist.

**Wedge.** Non-California GCs (SiteVetter's gap), OSHA-first and nationwide. SiteVetter can close that gap quickly. Positive side: a competitor at $79 shows willingness to pay. Market size is adequate (tens of thousands of small GCs), but it needs only ~4 accounts a month.

## B3: Recipe-card heirloom cookbook (score 6)

**Demand signals.** This is an evergreen, paid-social gift category:
- Storyworth is $99–109; sources conflict on whether $99 covers two hardcover credits ([Storyworth](https://welcome.storyworth.com/storyworth-pricing)).
- Create My Cookbook sells "snap photos of handwritten cards, we type them".
- Many Etsy custom recipe-book listings exist, including Star Sellers ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4414325047)).

**Competitors (~15+):**
- **Create My Cookbook:** Family Cookbook Kit **$29.95** (marked down from $49.95), including 30 human-typed recipes and a 50-page softcover; holiday kits include 100 credits ([CMC](https://createmycookbook.com/c/family-cookbook-kit-gift)).
- **Premium designer service:** EverPresent, which already does scanned-card facsimile books ([EverPresent](https://everpresent.com/cookbook-design-service/)).
- **Other:** TasteBook.
- **AI-native:** Old Family Recipe (AI transcription plus printing, explicitly pitched as a Mother's Day gift) ([PeerPush](https://peerpush.com/p/old-family-recipe)), Cookfolio AI, ReciNote, Heirloom Kitchen and ReciScan.

**Implication.** At $119, B3 is 4× the cheapest human-transcription incumbent. Its creative must sell the **facsimile-plus-typeset hardcover** as premium, against EverPresent's designer tier.

**Saturation:** Medium-High. **AI risk:** Medium. Transcription is commoditized; the paid channel and the physical book are the edge.

**Market size.** Ample. 30–50 Q4 orders is a tiny share of the category.

**Wedge.** "Done for you in one upload" as an instant gift certificate. The Christmas-now, book-in-January structure sidesteps print cutoffs that competitors face. That timing advantage is real.

## P1: Merchant-loaded ATMs (score 4)

**Demand signals:**
- Surcharges are at record highs: the deployer average is about $3.15, and ~$3.22 per the ideator's 2026 source ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/as-out-of-network-atm-fees-climb-to-an-all-time-high-cash-gets-more-expensive/); [beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/14/atm-vault-cash-surcharge-bookkeeping-interchange-reconciliation-guide)).
- A typical retail machine does 100–200 tx/mo; 150–300 is the industry-blog claim ([MoneyInc](https://moneyinc.com/how-much-can-a-local-atm-business-make-in-2026/)).

**Volume threshold.** ISOs want **250–300 tx for merchant-load** and 400+ elsewhere ([ATM Marketplace](https://www.atmmarketplace.com/news/return-of-the-placement/)). P1's 150-tx niche is "underserved" mostly because it is marginal.

**Interchange.** Mastercard's tiering now leaves low-volume acquirers ~$0.32 net ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/new-mastercard-atm-rates-will-crimp-income-for-banks-and-isos/)). The ideator's $0.10 is conservative, which is a small upside. But net interchange is down more than 25% over the long run.

**Competitors.** Hundreds+ ISOs and independents. Most cash-heavy shops already have a machine or have been pitched. Cash's share of payments is declining (14%, Fed 2026 Diary per the ideator).

**Saturation:** High. **AI risk:** Low.

**Market size.** Enough shops exist, but finding ones above 150 tx that lack an ATM is the hard part.

**Wedge.** A higher merchant split (40%) at shops where an ATM was recently pulled. It is a real but narrow wedge, and Lob response rates are unproven.

## P2: Power-bank rental stations (score 4)

**Demand signals.**
- **Brick:** about **1 rental/day typical**, 5–15 at strong venues, ~€2,500 a year per station ([Brick FAQ](https://articles.brick.tech/faq/)). That data is mostly European.
- **US:** ChargeFUZE charges $5/h (capped at $45) at large venues such as Legoland ([Legoland](https://florida-support.legoland.com/hc/en-us/articles/27431386071709-Do-you-have-cell-phone-charging-stations-available-for-guests)). Voltage Nexus began its **initial** US rollout on 13 Aug 2026 at $4/h, capped at $50, using a partner-owned model ([Vending Times](https://www.vendingtimes.com/news/voltage-nexus-launches-portable-battery-rental-kiosk-network/)). AnkerBox's US pilot failed in 2016.

**Competitors (~6–8):** ChargeFUZE, Brick, Voltage Nexus, Voozaa ($7.5k minimum), Recharge City and ichargepoint franchises.

**Supply-side pattern.** Most "competition" is franchise and biz-opp sellers recruiting station owners. That pattern usually signals that operator returns are oversold.

**Saturation:** Low at small bars, because the habit isn't established in the US. **AI risk:** Low.

**Market size.** Bars are plentiful. Utilisation is the unknown: the P50 of 20 rentals a month equals Brick's typical EU station divided by 1.5. That is plausible but unproven in US small bars.

**Wedge.** Small long-dwell venues skipped by ChargeFUZE. The pilot is the right test.

## P3: PlacementDesk pay-on-install locator (score 4)

**Demand signals.**
- Locators charge $399.99 to $1,111+ **upfront** ([search results](https://vendingmachinefree-com.lovable.app/vending-machine-cost)).
- A Fiverr cold-call location gig has 113 reviews at 4.5★ ([Fiverr](https://www.fiverr.com/s/o81wXkX)).
- Gumroad location guides sell.
- ATM Link runs a location-referral partnership programme ([ATM Marketplace](https://www.atmmarketplace.com/companies/showcases/atm-link-inc/products/unlock-your-earning-potential-with-atm-links-partnership-program)).

**Who actually pays.** Most of that spending comes from **new or aspiring operators**, the side-hustle crowd, whom P3 must exclude under the FTC Business Opportunity Rule. RouteConsultant says locators suit scaling operators ([RouteConsultant](https://routeconsultant.com/industry-insights/should-you-use-a-vending-locator-service)). Established operators' willingness to pay $250 per install is unverified; some prospect in-house.

**Competitors.** Hundreds+ locators and Fiverr gigs, plus AI SDR tools.

**Saturation:** High. **AI risk:** High, because AI cold outreach is the commodity input.

**Market size.** About 1.6 installs a month needs perhaps 2–3 active operator partners. That is feasible if they exist, but expect a 5–10% yes-rate (§14).

**Wedge.** Pay-on-install with no upfront fee, plus multi-category bundles at one host. The ATM ISO referral programmes are the most concrete buyer.

## U5: AI-managed vending with outsourced restocking (score 3)

**Demand signals.** Vending is a huge category. A 2026 route blend is ~$400–700/mo gross per machine; low-traffic sites gross $300–700 and high-traffic sites $1,500–3,000+ ([vmfsusa](https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/starting-your-vending-business/how-much-can-a-vending-machine-really-make)). Typical profit after COGS (40–50%) and commission (15–25%) is about $300–500 per machine **before outsourced labour**. Weekly paid restocking at $25–40 per visit takes ~$100–170/mo of that.

**Competitors.** Thousands+: local routes, national micro-market operators and a large wave of side-hustle entrants (unverified as a 2026 trend; the search didn't surface data).

**Micro-markets.** They are taking the best (50+ staff) offices: 43.5k markets by 2020, with vending machines down 21%, and replacements lift sales up to 80% ([Nayax/industry summaries](https://www.nayax.com/blog/workplace-micro-market)). New 1–3-machine entrants are left with the long tail of weak locations.

**Saturation:** Red ocean for locations. **AI risk:** Low.

**Market size.** Not the issue. Location quality and labour-adjusted margin are.

**Wedge.** Underserved shift-work sites (warehouses, auto shops, apartment clubhouses) that micro-markets skip. Even there, outsourced restocking compresses net to roughly $100–250 per machine, so 2–3 good machines are needed. Sourcing could be paired with P3, but that is correlated risk.

### Cross-idea observations
1. **B2 has a near-identical live competitor using the same product name.** This should be raised with the committee and the legal-risk analyst (renaming plus California exclusion).
2. **Q2 economics may be understated.** TrustMRR labels the revenue as after store fees. Confirm this in due diligence; even so, payback is ~15–16 months.
3. **Physical ideas (P1, P2, U5) have Low AI risk but High or unproven demand risk.** Their moat is real; their market is either saturated (ATM, vending) or unestablished in the US (power banks).
4. **B1's timing thesis is weakened.** HSK 3.0 launched on 1 Jul 2026, and AI-built speaking-mock apps (HanSpik and others) already exist with little traction.
